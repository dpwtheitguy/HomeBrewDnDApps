# D&D merchant research and inventory brief

Research pass dated 2026-11-12. The register contains 94 rows covering named businesses, markets, shopkeepers, trade houses, mobile merchants, and purchase services for a future Arms and Equipment Guide prototype. Geography, ownership, stock, prices, and available cash can each have different evidence. The app already has a six-item Blinsky shop. This brief leaves app implementation for a later request.

## Reading the register

- **Published** means the named entity was found in the cited game publication. **Fan** means the source is an attributed expansion. **Lead** means a citation points to material that still needs primary-source confirmation. **Generic** means a proposed shop or stock model.
- Each row has an estimated cash/stock tier unless a published figure is stated. **Small** means about 25–100 gp of buying cash and 100–400 gp of on-hand stock at base value. **Established** means 100–500 gp cash and 500–2,000 gp stock. **Specialist** means 500–2,000 gp cash and 2,000–10,000 gp stock. **Trade house** means a locally accessible branch might have 2,000–10,000 gp cash and 10,000–50,000 gp of contracted goods, subject to DM approval. These are tabletop design estimates. Cash is the amount available to purchase from players, stock is the value of sellable goods, and neither measures the merchant’s total wealth. A market has independent stall limits. Services, barter, and prices quoted in older editions can override the tier.
- Printed page numbers are used where given. Preserve the original currency, quantity, units, and price basis when recording a rule. A fee from an adventure should remain visible when an adapted D&D 2024 market option is enabled.

### Darokin and Minrothad trade networks

GAZ11 distinguishes merchants, retailers, caravans, and wholesale trade. A trade house can supply multiple shops and caravan stops. House wealth rankings describe the organization, while buying cash belongs to its branch or caravan. Pennydown is a particularly useful scale test. Its nearly 100 shops are published as a count; branch names and inventories require separate sources or generated entries labeled accordingly. ## Generic stores and pricing for the prototype

Provide low-cost, standard, and premium filters independently of shop type, locality, and wealth. A poor district can have a modestly priced specialist. A rich city can contain a cheap provisions stall. Estimate prices using the 2024 equipment base cost in integer copper, retaining the native game or edition price separately. Filter on the final price. A higher player budget should leave the merchant’s inventory and stable item IDs intact. [D&D 2024 Equipment](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/equipment)

| Thyatis City generic example | Regular stock | Rotating or order-only stock | Draft economics |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low-cost harbor outfitter | Rope, lights, repair cord, needles, rations, sacks | Used tools, tack, ordinary work clothes, imports suited to the port | 12–24 distinct items, Small liquidity, typical list prices ×0.90–1.10 |
| Standard market outfitter | Pack and camp goods, tools, travel clothes, ammunition, ordinary weapons if licensed | Shields and armor, specialist kits, regional imports | 20–40 distinct items, Established liquidity, typical list prices ×1.00–1.20 |
| Premium guild armorer | Higher-quality armor and metalwork, secure fittings, house commissions | Decorated work, rare materials, fitted plate by order; magic only when the campaign allows | 12–30 distinct items, Specialist liquidity, typical list prices ×1.15–1.60 |

Apply a visible setting overlay to the template. Threshold favors trail and river supplies, repairs, and tavern services. Darokin favors consignments, textiles, food, and shop networks. Minrothad adds maritime suppliers and guild wares. Thunder Rift uses delivery delays and local makers. Barovia favors scarce everyday goods and supply-state restrictions. Waterdeep adds ward-specific guild services, and Baldur’s Gate separates the regulated city from Outer City markets. Published rules take precedence over generic price bands, and magic stock needs explicit source or DM permission.

For each price, apply a fixed published price or fixed market multiplier first, then a documented scenario or regional adjustment where allowed, then an optional campaign bargaining policy. Round once to integer copper. Keep the price used for eligibility separate from what the player pays. A 25 gp baseline item is eligible at the Stockyard and excluded at Bildrath’s; the Stockyard price is 125 gp. Item types must match the published Adventuring Gear table even when a catalog search classifies an item under additional filters. Map eligibility from a versioned snapshot of the original table and document unmapped equivalents. Merchant buyback should use the buying cash left after prior transactions, acceptable categories, and a configurable offer percentage. Optional Charisma and attitude rules can alter eligible prices or offers while honoring no-haggle and fixed-price policies. Preserve noncoin costs, deposits, and lead times.

## Data model for later implementation

| Record | Fields to start with |
| --- | --- |
| Setting, region, settlement | Stable IDs, aliases, parent IDs, named continuity and era, settlement size source and reference date, market scale, trade routes, cultures, source references |
| Merchant identity | Stable entity ID, display name, kind, names and aliases, keepers and ownership over time, parent trade house, shared warehouse, physical site or route-stop IDs, culture/faction tags, published/fan/generic status |
| Offer | Stable offer ID, item catalog ID or service ID, direction, quantity, costs, conditions, eligibility and original price table, fixed/rolling status, provenance, revision history |

Keep the merchant register as a shared catalog supplement. A source can verify identity without establishing prices. Track explicit publication, interpretation of publication, fan report, and estimate independently for each field. Quotes, sales, barter, purchase orders, services, and quest rewards need distinct offer types. The player drill-down should expose published stock and price evidence, estimated stock, alternative names, location, and current availability. Keep plot secrets and holdings for DM view. Reserve and route information can be shared across a house’s branches while local availability varies.

## Android date and gradual rotation

- Android’s local calendar date drives generation offline. Derive a day number from the civil date, avoiding a 24-hour duration from the last visit. Read the date again when resuming the app and preserve a visit snapshot while the user has an active basket. A campaign can choose the tablet’s zone or pin a named zone for a group. Store the zone, date, campaign seed, generator version, and catalog version in snapshots.
- Keep published retail, recurring staples, and story items in fixed slots. Seed each optional slot from a stable hash of campaign ID, merchant ID, slot ID, seed version, and that slot’s current window. Give slots staggered offsets across a 14–28 day cycle. A target of 75–90% stable lines over a seven-day comparison is reasonable for general stock. Use smaller special pools with fixed starter lines for thin inventories. Allow daily variation in a few eligible quantities or temporary specials, and publish route arrival cadence independently.
- Select candidates from sorted stable catalog IDs and use a versioned deterministic generator, for example SHA-256 seed plus a specified PRNG. Include edition, location, quality, inventory cost and restricted tags before drawing. Use distinct picks, reserve stock value for staples, and decline offers that exceed the shop’s stock cap. Persist realized assignments for surviving slots so unrelated inventory or catalog edits leave existing stock stable. Apply catalog updates on a known refresh boundary and record changed offers.
- Persist purchases and pending orders. Reopening, restarting, changing filters, shifting local time, or setting the date back must preserve purchased quantities. Offline conflicts between tablets require optional shared campaign sync. The same seed and date can reproduce opening stock before transactions. Define restocking as a stock event, with identical event IDs applied once.
- Test same-day repeatability, daylight saving and year boundaries, missing dates or clocks going backward, one-week overlap, stock caps, distinct picks, no-haggle, the 25 gp threshold, fixed Blinsky lines, barter, campaign secrecy, catalog upgrades, and settlement alias stability. Use separate compact and wide Android acceptance checks.
